Q1. Soundness

(a) Equal 0.20 weighting is not defensible as the primary decision structure.

It implies compensability between dimensions that are not actually interchangeable. Capability and reliability are hard constraints, not weighted preferences. A solution failing either gate cannot become acceptable by scoring highly on cost, time, or intervention potential.

Use a lexicographic objective instead:

Satisfy capability ≥ threshold.

Satisfy reliability ≥ threshold.

Among feasible solutions, minimize time and cost (with an explicit trade-off rule).

Use intervention potential as a secondary criterion for improving feasibility.

Equal weights can remain as descriptive dashboard weights, but not as the optimization function.

(b) The five dimensions are not orthogonal.

There is a category error:

Capability and reliability: outcome dimensions.

Time and cost: resource/objective dimensions.

Intervention levers: controllable inputs.

Furthermore, reliability improvements often consume time and cost; interventions affect all four other dimensions. Treating them as independent dimensions risks double-counting.

Fix: Separate the model into:

Decision variables / interventions.

Performance outcomes.

Resource constraints.

Feasibility gates.

Represent causal dependencies explicitly, preferably through a DAG.

(c) S-a/S-b/S-c is useful but insufficient.

The classification distinguishes evidence acquisition requirements, not evidence quality. An S-a calculation can still inherit unreliable assumptions; an S-b experiment can have poor statistical power.

Reporting unknowns for S-c is epistemically sound, but stopping there is under-committed for a decision-oriented research project.

Two improvements:

Add evidence-quality fields: provenance, measurement uncertainty, sample size, reproducibility.

For S-c, report an interval or sensitivity range, identify the decision threshold, and specify what observation would resolve the uncertainty.

Q2. Causal re-attribution

Yes. The correction is both correct and important.

The two numbers estimate different contrasts:

+0.182: S5 − S0, measuring the prompt-level ablation difference between structured pipeline and bare generation (n=15 per arm, local judge).

+0.025: The SM pipeline arm's own measured effect, with p≈0.32 and no statistically significant result.

Attributing +0.182 to the SM arm conflates two experimental contrasts. It is a treatment-attribution error, not merely a labeling inconsistency.

Using +0.182 as the SM arm's effect downstream would be a material error.

One qualification: p≈0.32 does not establish that the SM effect is zero. It establishes that this experiment does not provide sufficient evidence to distinguish its measured effect from zero at conventional significance levels.

Required correction: Preserve +0.182 under the prompt-level ablation comparison; attribute +0.025 to the SM arm. Do not transfer the former estimate into the latter's causal evidence or downstream projections.
