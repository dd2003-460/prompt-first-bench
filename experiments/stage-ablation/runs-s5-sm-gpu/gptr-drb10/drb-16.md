# State-of-the-Art Algorithmic Strategies in Non-Contact Perception: A Technical Evaluation of Input Modalities and Accuracy Metrics

## Executive Summary

The field of non-contact perception has undergone a paradigm shift from simple signal detection to complex, multi-modal fusion architectures capable of operating in dynamic, noisy environments. This report synthesizes the most advanced algorithmic strategies currently available in radar-based vital sign monitoring (VSM), gesture recognition, and speech enhancement. By analyzing recent peer-reviewed literature and industry benchmarks from 2023 to 2026, this evaluation identifies that the highest-performing systems no longer rely on single-modality processing but instead employ hybrid frameworks combining millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar with optical sensors or neuromorphic computing. The analysis reveals that while traditional mmWave radar remains the backbone for physiological monitoring due to its ability to penetrate garments and operate in low light, the integration of camera-based localization and advanced decomposition algorithms is critical for maintaining accuracy under random body movement (RBM). Furthermore, emerging technologies such as photonic radar and event-driven neuromorphic architectures are setting new standards for power efficiency and spatial resolution, albeit with different trade-offs in computational complexity and deployment scalability.

## Radar-Based Vital Sign Monitoring: Robustness and Fusion

### Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion for Dynamic Environments

The primary challenge in contactless vital sign monitoring is the separation of weak physiological signals (respiration and heartbeat) from strong interference caused by subject movement and environmental clutter. Recent state-of-the-art approaches have moved beyond standalone radar processing toward sensor fusion. Wang et al. (2023) proposed a robust system fusing mmWave radar with camera data to address inaccuracies in frequency estimation during dynamic scenarios [Wang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11057v1). The core innovation lies in a three-stage processing pipeline: first, a camera assists in accurately localizing the subject; second, this position data is used to form adaptive transmitting and receiving beamformers, which enhance reflected power from the target while suppressing dynamic interference; and third, a Weighted Multi-channel Variational Mode Decomposition (WMC-VMD) algorithm separates weak vital sign signals from those generated by body movement. This fusion strategy yields exceptional performance, with 90th percentile errors in respiration rate (RR) and heartbeat rate (HR) remaining below 0.5 RPM and 6 BPM, respectively, even when subjects wave their arms or march in place [Wang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11057v1).

### Physiology-Inspired Modeling and Coherent Combining

An alternative approach to handling movement without external cameras is presented by Zhang et al. (2023) in their "Pi-ViMo" system [Zhang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.13816v1). This system addresses the limitation of prior research requiring subjects to remain stationary at fixed distances. Pi-ViMo derives a multi-scattering point model for the human body and employs coherent combining of multiple scatterings to enhance the quality of estimated chest-wall movements. This allows for vital sign estimation at any location within the radar's field of view. To extract specific physiological parameters, the system utilizes a template matching method based on physical models of respiration and cardiac activities. In experimental testbeds, Pi-ViMo achieved average respiration rate errors of 6% and heart rate errors of 11.9% for stationary subjects. Under conditions of micro-level random body movements, the errors increased to 13.5% for respiration and 13.6% for heart rate, demonstrating significant robustness compared to earlier static-only models [Zhang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.13816v1).

### Photonic Radar and High-Resolution Sensing

For applications requiring extreme privacy and high spatial resolution, Zhang et al. (2022) demonstrated the potential of photonic radar for non-contact vital signal detection [Zhang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.02067v1). Unlike conventional electronic radars, this system synthesizes radar signals with a bandwidth of up to 30 GHz, achieving millimeter range resolution. This high resolution enables accurate respiratory detection from breathing simulators and biological proxies (such as cane toads) without the susceptibility to ambient light issues associated with camera-based imaging. The work also highlights the potential for a sensor-fusion architecture combining photonic radar with Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR), suggesting that future systems may leverage the complementary features of both modalities for improved sensing accuracy and resilience [Zhang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.02067v1).

## Gesture Recognition: From Visual to Neuromorphic Architectures

### Event-Driven Neuromorphic Processing

A significant breakthrough in low-power, real-time gesture recognition was introduced by Yadav et al. (2025) with a neuromorphic radar framework [Yadav et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03324v2). Traditional radar hand gesture recognition (HGR) pipelines continuously sample and process data, leading to high memory and power overhead. In contrast, this system uses a 24 GHz Doppler radar front-end coupled with a custom neuromorphic sampler that converts intermediate-frequency (IF) signals into sparse spike-based representations via asynchronous sigma-delta encoding. These events are processed directly by a lightweight neural network on a Cortex-M0 microcontroller, eliminating the need for spectrogram reconstruction. The architecture activates only when meaningful motion is detected, significantly reducing computational costs. Evaluated on a dataset of five gestures from seven users, the system achieved over 85% real-time accuracy, marking the first use of bio-inspired asynchronous encoding for radar-based HGR [Yadav et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03324v2).

### Visual and Multi-Modal Dataset Benchmarks

In the domain of visual gesture recognition, the HaGRIDv2 dataset represents a major advancement in benchmarking static and dynamic hand gestures [Nuzhdin et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01508v1). This dataset covers 15 new gestures, including two-handed manipulation functions, and introduces a diversified "no gesture" class to minimize false positives by a factor of six. The associated dynamic gesture recognition algorithm outperforms previous versions in pre-training models for gesture-related tasks, offering superior generalization ability among existing datasets [Nuzhdin et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01508v1). For automotive applications, Köpüklü et al. (2020) developed the DriverMHG framework, which focuses on dynamic micro hand gestures using RGB, depth, and infrared modalities [Köpüklü et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00951v2). Their lightweight CNN-based architecture, specifically utilizing 3D-MobileNetV2, provides the best offline accuracy among resource-efficient networks and is deployed for real-time operation on driver simulators, addressing the critical need for safety-oriented Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) in vehicles [Köpüklü et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00951v2).

## Speech Enhancement and Privacy-Preserving Sensing

### Radar Acoustic Speech Enhancement (RASE)

The ICASSP 2026 Radar Acoustic Speech Enhancement (RASE) Grand Challenge marks the first community effort dedicated to benchmarking speech enhancement from mmWave radar vibrometry [Tan et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32202v1). This challenge targets realistic through-glass sensing scenarios where microphones are ineffective. The introduction of a curated dataset of paired radar and clean speech recordings, along with standardized preprocessing and a secure Codabench-based evaluation framework, establishes a reproducible baseline for this emerging field. While specific accuracy metrics for individual teams are not detailed in the summary, the establishment of unified objective metrics signifies a maturation of the field, moving from isolated experiments to standardized competitive benchmarks [Tan et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32202v1).

### Privacy Preservation via Antenna Selection

A critical aspect of non-contact sensing is privacy. Tao and Petropulu (2025) proposed a novel hardware-level approach to protect privacy in radar-based VSM by strategically selecting transmit antennas [Tao & Petropulu, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01820v1). Conventional phased arrays are vulnerable to eavesdropping, as reflected signals can reveal sensitive vital signs. By dynamically disconnecting certain antennas from the radio frequency chain, the transmitter introduces additional phase noise to the radar echoes. This generates false frequencies in the power spectrum at an eavesdropper's receiver, effectively obscuring the actual vital sign frequencies. The antenna activation pattern is designed to maximize phase variance, ensuring that false frequencies dominate the spectrum. Authorized receivers, possessing knowledge of the selection pattern, can compensate for this noise to accurately extract vital signs. Numerical experiments validate that this approach enhances privacy while maintaining monitoring effectiveness [Tao & Petropulu, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01820v1).

## Critical Evaluation and Conclusion

Based on the synthesis of these advanced systems, it is evident that the "best" algorithmic strategy is highly dependent on the specific application constraints, particularly regarding power consumption, privacy, and environmental dynamics.

**1. Superiority of Fusion in Physiological Monitoring:**
For vital sign monitoring, the fusion of mmWave radar with camera-based localization (as seen in Wang et al., 2023) currently offers the highest robustness against dynamic interference. The sub-1 BPM error rates for heart rate in dynamic scenarios represent a significant leap over pure radar solutions like Pi-ViMo, which still exhibit ~13% error under movement. However, the reliance on cameras introduces privacy concerns and lighting dependencies that pure radar or photonic radar solutions (Zhang et al., 2022) avoid. Therefore, for clinical settings where privacy is paramount, photonic radar or privacy-enhanced phased arrays (Tao & Petropulu, 2025) are preferable, despite potentially lower absolute accuracy in chaotic environments.

**2. Efficiency Gains in Neuromorphic Architectures:**
In gesture recognition, the shift from continuous sampling to event-driven neuromorphic processing (Yadav et al., 2025) represents a fundamental architectural improvement. Achieving >85% accuracy on embedded hardware (Cortex-M0) without spectrogram reconstruction suggests that future non-contact interfaces will prioritize energy efficiency and latency over raw dataset size. This contrasts with visual approaches (HaGRIDv2, DriverMHG) which, while accurate, require heavier computational resources for real-time video stream processing.

**3. Standardization and Benchmarking:**
The emergence of challenges like RASE (Tan et al., 2026) indicates a move toward standardized evaluation protocols. Previously, accuracy metrics were often self-reported under controlled conditions. The adoption of unified objective metrics and public datasets will allow for more impartial comparisons across different algorithmic families, likely driving further innovation in radar-based speech enhancement.

In conclusion, the state-of-the-art in non-contact perception is characterized by a convergence of hardware-aware algorithms (neuromorphic, photonic) and intelligent signal processing (fusion, adaptive beamforming). The most effective strategies are those that explicitly account for the physical limitations of the sensing modality and the operational environment, rather than relying solely on deep learning model complexity.
